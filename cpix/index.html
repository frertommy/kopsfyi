How it works

Many markets, one signal.

01

Each market is a noisy read

Headline CPI, core CPI and core PCE each price a slightly different slice of inflation — and each is a noisy estimate of the same underlying force. Producer prices lead them all.

02

Pool them into one factor

We combine the inflation-rate markets into a single number, core-weighted so it tracks the persistent trend rather than the gas-driven monthly swings. Pooling cancels the noise any one market carries.

03

Robust & all-Kalshi

Because it's a blend, no single thin or jumpy market can throw it off. Producer prices (pipeline) and the current monthly pace are shown alongside as context, not folded into the level.

CPIX is the underlying-inflation sibling of CPIPERP. CPIPERP tracks the official headline print; CPIX strips the monthly noise to show the persistent trend — both built live from Kalshi.

Why now

Inflation is more than one print.

The monthly CPI print gets all the attention — and it's noisy. A jump in gasoline or airfares can swing the headline in a way that says nothing about where inflation is really heading. So the most-watched number in markets is also one of the jumpiest.

CPIX reads the whole inflation complex at once — headline, core, producer prices, the monthly pace — and folds them into one underlying rate. Each market carries part of the signal and some noise; pooled together, the noise cancels and the trend remains. One number, built entirely from live prediction markets, that answers what inflation is actually running at.

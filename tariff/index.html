Index methodology

Treasury receipts, not a survey.

01

Straight from Treasury

The Treasury reports customs duties collected every business day. This is the actual money taken in — days ahead of any monthly trade report.

02

Turned into a rate

Customs collected, divided by the value of goods imported, gives the effective tariff rate the US is actually charging right now.

03

The Kalshi signal check

Kalshi's tariff markets price where the rate will land. The gap between the Kalshi signal and the receipts data is the signal — there's no other market that prices this directly.

Why now

What tariffs actually cost.

Tariffs are reshaping trade in real time — and the official effective rate is buried in monthly data that lands weeks late. In between, people argue from headline rates and announcements that may never be collected. Each is a proxy; none is the cash actually hitting Treasury.

So the one number that says what tariffs are really costing arrives last.

TARIFF reads the Daily Treasury Statement — the customs duties deposited every business day — and turns them into a live effective-rate read, cross-checked against the Kalshi signal. No derivatives market prices tariffs, so this receipts-vs-Kalshi gap is the only real-time read there is.

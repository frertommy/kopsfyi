The four bases

Every hedge leaves a residue.

A hedge converts price risk into basis risk. Each leg below is one flavor of that residue, measured — not theorized.

01

Settlement basis

Dispersion of independent H100 benchmark prints — Ornn (transacted), CCIR T2/T3 (reference), ComputePrices (list median). If the indexes disagree, whichever one your contract settles to defines your gap. –

02

Obsolescence basis

Annualized drift of the cross-vintage price ratios (B200/H100, H200/H100, A100/H100). The unanchored leg — no trade converts an H100-hour into a B200-hour. –

03

Quality basis

Average pairwise 30-day correlation of daily moves across SKUs. Can an H100 contract hedge an H200 book? Cross-SKU hedge effectiveness, updated daily. –

04

Calendar basis

Realized error of CCIR's committed 3-month quotes vs the spot that later printed — does the forward curve predict, or just charge a premium? –

CIB = 100 × mean( settlement, obsolescence, quality, calendar )
each leg normalized so a WTI-like market scores ≈ 85 and gold ≈ 95 — anchors published, methodology fixed.

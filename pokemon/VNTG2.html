01Why a Gen-2

Gen-1 PKMN-VNTG is a dollar-volume-screened benchmark of the WOTC era. But Vintage is the hard case — thin, high-value, infrequently-traded, ~2.5× Modern's volatility — and that is precisely the asset profile that real-estate and art-market quants spent 60 years building tools for. Gen-2 brings the full kit: Case-Shiller repeat-sales, the Mei-Moses art index, Geltner unsmoothing, Asness on volatility laundering, vol-control overlays.

Vintage's wildness isn't a bug to hide — it's a risk to budget. Gen-2 measures it honestly, then normalizes it into something a perp can actually carry.

02Fundamental × Confidence weighting

Like its Modern sibling, Vintage Gen-2 severs weight from price (Arnott) and tilts toward priceability. The confidence leg matters most here, because Vintage is where fragile prices live — 35% of components are "thin," 27% trade on a single venue.

w(i) ∝ fundamental(i)^0.8 · confidence(i)
confidence = min(1, clusters/3) · 1/(1+dispersion) · firmness
cap: 8% card · 25% franchise · ≤ tradeable-float share
Validated. On live data the confidence tilt cut Vintage's realized volatility ~24% (31.7% → 24.0%) — by down-weighting fragile single-venue/noisy names (e.g. a 1-cluster Neo Umbreon dropped from 2.0%→0.4%) while keeping well-confirmed icons (Base Charizard, multi-venue 1st-editions) dominant. The biggest single-sleeve quality win we measured.

03Hedonic fair-value + Case-Shiller cross-check

The thin Vintage tape is exactly where a smoothed-median mark fails — most cards don't sell on most days. Gen-2 uses a forward-only hedonic-imputation mark (uses every sale, no revision, published SE) over the observable characteristics — grade, set, 1st-edition/shadowless, character, pop — and settles on auction-preferred hammer prices from PWCC / Goldin / Heritage / eBay, which we now ingest as independent venues.

Offline, a Case-Shiller repeat-sales index (BMN with the 3-stage heteroskedasticity GLS that down-weights thin, wide-gap pairs — built for exactly this) validates the live mark and detects mix-bias. Feasibility confirmed: ~3,400 vintage return-pairs.

The masterpiece check. In art, the priciest works underperform per dollar (Mei-Moses). We quantify the same for PSA-10 Base Charizard / Moonbreon-class names — and the fundamental+cap weighting already leans against over-loading them.

04Smoothing done right — cross-sectional, not time-series

A fixed time-series smoother makes any thin index an appraisal-based one: it understates volatility and manufactures autocorrelation (Geltner). We confirmed the fixed 14-day window under-smooths Vintage (it trades too rarely for 14d to stabilize) — one global constant can't serve a sleeve this sparse.

The fix: control noise cross-sectionally (winsorized cross-card median) rather than via a time-series filter, and where a per-card window is needed, self-tune it to each card's own next-sale prediction error (beat fixed-14d ~18% out-of-sample) — which for Vintage often means not simply "smooth more."

05The volatility-target overlay — and true vol

This is Vintage Gen-2's headline. Raw Vintage runs ~2.5× Modern's vol, which makes it punishing to margin and prone to liquidation cascades as a perp. A volatility-target overlay scales exposure to a constant risk budget:

exposure(t) = target_vol / realized_vol(t) realized_vol = blend(20d, 60d), capped at max leverage

Vol-targeting publishes a PKMN-VNTG2-RC whose realized vol matches Modern's — so both perps share margin and funding behavior, and cross-hedge cleanly, even though raw Vintage is far wilder.

And because all of this is smoothed for display, Gen-2 also publishes the Geltner-unsmoothed true vol — the number funding and margin actually run on — pre-empting the "volatility laundering" critique (Asness). Cross-correlation to Modern is Dimson-corrected for non-synchronous trading before any hedge ratio is quoted; the famous "≈ 0" may partly be an artifact, and we test it honestly.

06Liquidity screen & continuity

  • Dollar-volume screen (Vintage's value lives in low-frequency, high-value icons, so it's ranked by traded value, not trade count) plus a tradeable-float weight (pop × turnover).
  • Divisor method on every reconstitution → the level never gaps; buffer-banding (110%/90%) + staggered rebalance → low turnover and reduced front-running of the (very-alive, in this retail market) index effect.

07The swing-priced settlement oracle

Thin markets invite oracle manipulation — and Vintage is the thinnest. The Gen-2 oracle fuses fair-value (winsorized, cluster-capped — a wash trade can't print), a TWAP/recency window (one sale can't set settlement), and an anti-dilution swing that biases the mark against whoever's pushing flow. TradFi swing pricing meets DeFi oracle defense — manipulation costs the manipulator.

08Volatility as yield

A disciplined rebalanced index harvests a diversification return (Fernholz SPT) that grows with the universe's volatility and diversity. Vintage is the most volatile sleeve — so it harvests the most.

Vintage's wildness, which the vol-target overlay tames on the front end, becomes yield on the back end — routed to LPs/stakers, not paid in token emissions.

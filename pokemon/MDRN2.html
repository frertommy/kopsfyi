01Why a Gen-2

Gen-1 PKMN-MDRN is a clean, liquidity-screened, dollar-volume-capped benchmark — respectable, and essentially MSCI/S&P investability logic. But a card index is an ETF on an illiquid, heterogeneous, infrequently-traded asset, and that exact problem has been solved — by real-estate and art quants — since the 1960s. Gen-2 stands on that canon: Case-Shiller, the Mei-Moses art index, Geltner return-unsmoothing, Asness on volatility laundering, Arnott's fundamental indexing, Choueifaty diversification.

Gen-1 answers "where does money trade?" Gen-2 also answers "where can we price it un-gameably, and what is the true risk?" — the questions that matter once an index settles a leveraged perp.

02Fundamental × Confidence weighting

Dollar-volume (and market-cap) weighting has a structural flaw Arnott named: weight is mechanically tied to price, so the index over-weights whatever is currently expensive — a real drag in a hype-driven market full of Moonbreon-style manias. Gen-2 severs weight from price.

w(i) ∝ fundamental(i)^0.8 · confidence(i)
fundamental = f( 1/pop-scarcity , trailing velocity , venue-breadth ) // RAFI-on-cards, non-price
confidence = min(1, clusters/3) · 1/(1+dispersion) · firmness // can we trust the price?
cap: 8% card · 25% franchise · ≤ tradeable-float share (iterative)

The fundamental leg weights by a card's economic footprint — graded-population scarcity, sales velocity, venue breadth — data we uniquely hold, not its momentary price. The confidence leg down-weights names whose price we can't trust (single-venue, high-dispersion, thin).

Validated. On live data the confidence tilt shifts weight off fragile single-venue / high-dispersion names toward multi-venue, tight-dispersion ones — Modern's icons (Moonbreon, Base-era SIRs) stay dominant but trimmed, and effective breadth roughly doubles. Modern's realized vol barely moves (its prices are already clean) — the gain here is manipulation-resistance and breadth, not vol.

03The hedonic fair-value settlement mark

Gen-1 marks each card on a smoothed median of its own sales — which throws away every card that didn't sell that day. Gen-2 uses a forward-only hedonic-imputation index: model price as a function of observable characteristics and extract a constant-quality level.

Pokémon is the hedonic dream case — every price-determining attribute (grade, set, rarity, character, era, language, population) is discrete and already in our schema. No unobserved "neighbourhood quality."

  • Uses every sale, not just same-day prints — decisive for breadth.
  • Forward-only: never revises a price we already settled a contract on.
  • Ships a published standard error per index point — transparency a counterparty can audit.
  • No-trade names get a cluster/grade factor-drag (move them by their cohort's return), never a stale last price.
  • Settles on auction-preferred hammer prices — actual clearing, no BIN/ask markup.

Repeat-sales (Case-Shiller GLS) runs offline as the academic cross-check and mix-bias detector.

04Smoothing done right — cross-sectional, not time-series

This is the subtle one, and the one a sharp quant checks first. A fixed time-series smoother (Gen-1's 14-day window) is functionally an appraisal-based index: smoothing returns before chaining them mechanically understates volatility and manufactures positive autocorrelation (Geltner). We proved 14d over-smooths Modern: prediction error rose with window length (7d beat 14d beat 30d beat 60d).

The fix: control noise cross-sectionally (winsorized cross-card median per day) instead of with a time-series filter — cross-sectional shrinkage kills daily noise without inducing serial correlation. Where a per-card window is still needed, it's self-tuned per card to minimize that card's own next-sale prediction error (beat fixed-14d by ~18% out-of-sample), not a global constant.

05True (unsmoothed) volatility

Any smoothed illiquid index reports artificially low vol — Asness calls it "volatility laundering." For a perp it's not cosmetic: the autocorrelation is predictable momentum we manufactured, and funding/margin would be calibrated to a fake-low number. Gen-2 publishes both:

r_true(t) = ( r_obs(t) − α·r_obs(t-1) ) / ( 1 − α ) α = observed AR(1) (Geltner / Blundell-Ward)
  • A display series (smooth) and an unsmoothed true-vol series — funding and margin run on the true one.
  • Cross-correlations to Vintage / crypto-beta are Dimson lead-lag corrected for non-synchronous trading before any hedge ratio is quoted.

Saying this before the room does turns the first critique a quant reaches for into a competence signal.

06Liquidity screen & continuity

  • ATVR-style screen (MSCI): a traded-value ratio plus a frequency-of-trading floor (must trade in ≥X of last Y weeks across ≥2 venues) — kills the single-whale-sale false positive.
  • Tradeable-float weighting: pop × turnover, not raw population — most of a card's pop sits in vaults and never circulates.
  • Divisor method on every reconstitution → the level never gaps at a membership change (no unfair liquidation).
  • Buffer-banding (enter at 110%, exit at 90% of threshold) + staggered/randomized rebalance → low turnover, and blunts front-running of the "index effect," which is likely alive in this retail market.

07The swing-priced settlement oracle

In thin markets the live risk is oracle manipulation — move a few prints, move the mark. Gen-2's settlement oracle fuses three defenses that no single field combines:

  • Fair-value, winsorized, cluster-capped marks (a wash trade can't print into the level);
  • a TWAP / recency window (one sale, one block, can't set settlement);
  • an anti-dilution swing (from open-end-fund swing pricing) that biases the mark against the side pushing flow — pushing the price costs the pusher.

TradFi has swing pricing but no adversarial on-chain actors; DeFi has TWAP oracles but no anti-dilution swing. The fusion is the point.

08Volatility as yield

A disciplined rebalanced, non-cap-weighted index harvests a diversification return (Fernholz's Stochastic Portfolio Theory: functionally-generated portfolios produce relative arbitrage when the market is diverse and volatile). Card markets are extreme-vol and diverse — so the premium is unusually large.

The more volatile the asset class, the more the index out-compounds its own constituents — a theory-backed native yield for LPs/stakers, not token emissions.

01What PKMN-SEALED is

PKMN-SEALED tracks the broad value of sealed Pokémon product — the boxes and packs people buy to rip or to hold. It is the third flagship alongside PKMN-MDRN and PKMN-VNTG, and it exists for one reason: it moves to a different drummer than the singles.

Singles are a scarcity asset — fixed, shrinking, graded supply. Sealed is a supply asset — still being printed, restocked, and ripped. Two opposite sides of the same equation.

That makes sealed a genuine diversifier: when reprints land or a set is over-allocated, sealed sags even as the singles inside hold or rise; when print is cut, sealed tightens. A hedge desk gets a third, lowly-correlated exposure instead of two views of the same chase-card demand.

Structurally modern. 123 of the 124 components are modern-era — because vintage sealed barely trades. A 1999 Base Set booster box is six figures and changes hands a few times a year; it can't clear the liquidity screen. So PKMN-SEALED is effectively modern sealed — and its diversification comes from sealed-vs-singles supply dynamics, not from era. The hottest constituents are the brand-new Mega Evolution era sets (ME01 onward: Chaos Rising, Phantasmal Flames, Ascended Heroes), where new-release velocity is highest.

03Selection

Same rule as the singles sleeves — the count falls out of liquidity, not a round number:

Hold the smallest set of liquid sealed products that covers 90% of the sleeve's trailing dollar volume.

That's 124 components (of ~350 eligible). The screen: ≥5 units sold / 30d, a live market price, real product (the catch-all "Miscellaneous" bucket is excluded). New sets enter only once they're actually trading at volume, not on announcement.

04Weighting

Dollar-volume weighted, capped — weight by the value that actually traded, so the index tracks where you can execute.

w(i) ∝ units(i) × price(i) (30d) , capped 8% per product, iterative

Result: top component ~8%, effective-N ≈ 39 — well diversified, no single box (or hyped new set) able to dominate the level. A per-set cap can be layered to stop one mega-release from running the index.

05The diversifier case

This is the whole point, so it gets measured, not asserted. Over the most recent window, sealed fell ~2.5% while modern singles rose ~4% — moving the opposite direction. That's the supply driver at work: new product over-allocated and post-release dumps weigh on boxes even as the chase singles inside stay bid.

Honest caveat: that's one window, and sealed vs singles correlation needs a real time series to nail (see §07). But the mechanism is structural — boxes are elastic, expanding supply; slabs are fixed, shrinking supply — so a low/negative correlation is what theory predicts, and the early data agrees. Low correlation is exactly what makes a third index worth holding rather than a redundant fourth view of the same demand.

06The Amazon velocity overlay

Sealed has a signal singles don't: retail availability and velocity. Amazon (via Keepa) reports lowest-new price, sales rank, and monthly units sold for ~700 sealed SKUs — Packs ~405 units/mo, Decks ~690. That's a soft price-ceiling and a demand-velocity gauge: when Amazon is in stock and cheap, it caps the secondary market; when it sells out, sealed tightens. It's an overlay (a leading supply/velocity signal), not a settlement source.

07Status & the next build

defined  Universe, screen, weighting and the 124-component basket are set and validated.

The one gap. Sealed is currently captured as a snapshot, not a continuous tape — so the index can be defined but not yet tracked (no live level chart, no proper realized-vol or sealed↔singles correlation). The next build is a daily sealed-price feed (TCGplayer sealed + StockX bid/ask + the Amazon/Keepa overlay) into the oracle. Once it accrues, PKMN-SEALED gets the same live level chart and risk stats as the singles indexes — and the diversifier thesis gets confirmed on real history instead of one window.

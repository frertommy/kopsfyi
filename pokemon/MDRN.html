01What PKMN-MDRN is

PKMN-MDRN is the S&P of modern Pokémon: one number that tracks the broad value of post-2016 graded chase cards. It is deliberately not a bet on any single card. It is a diversified, liquidity-screened basket designed so that a position in the index behaves like exposure to the asset class — and so that a short against it is a usable hedge for anyone long a pile of modern slabs.

The bar is not "perfect fair value per card." The bar is: track the hobby, move only when the hobby moves, and never lurch on one Charizard.

Everything below serves three properties the index is engineered for: (1) high correlation with the broad modern market, (2) controlled, explainable movement — no whipsaw on thin prints, and (3) no single card or character able to dominate the level.

02The universe — what counts as "Modern"

Era is assigned structurally from each card's set, not guessed from price. Modern is the 2016-and-later window — the three eras where modern liquidity actually lives:

  • SM — Sun & Moon (2017–2019)
  • SWSH — Sword & Shield (2020–2022) — home of Evolving Skies and the "Moonbreon" grail
  • SV — Scarlet & Violet (2023–present)

The pre-2016 XY / "mega" era (2014–2016) sits in an intentional gap — it is neither modern-liquid nor vintage, and including it would drag the index toward illiquid names. Modern is also explicitly the chase tier — alt arts, special illustration rares, secret/gold rares, full-art VMAX/VSTAR. Bulk commons inside the date window are filtered out by the liquidity screen below, not by hand.

Depth check: the modern universe contains ~1,920 cards with a live PSA-10 market and ~1,876 with a live PSA-9 market. Of those, 522 trade at least 20× every 60 days across two or more independent venues. Modern is not thin — the job of the index is to curate, not to scrape.

03Selection — why 173, and not a round number

A round component count ("top 100") is a tell that a human picked the number instead of the market. PKMN-MDRN uses one rule and lets the count fall out of it:

Hold the smallest set of liquid, recently-traded names that together cover 90% of the sleeve's trailing-60-day dollar volume.

For Modern that set is 173 names. (98 names cover 80% of dollar volume; 269 cover 95%.) 90% is the coverage line because 80% is too concentrated to be a market hedge, and 95% drags in a long thin tail that adds turnover and noise for the last sliver of volume. Anyone who asks "why 173?" gets a real answer: that's how many cards it takes to capture 90% of where modern money changes hands.

04Why Modern is built differently from Vintage

The screen above looks obvious until you try the same thing on Vintage and it breaks. That contrast is the single most important design insight in the whole program, so it's worth stating plainly:

Modern is a market of many liquid, mid-value names. Vintage is a market of a few high-value names that rarely trade.

In Modern, trade frequency and dollar volume agree — the cards that change hands most are also the valuable ones — so a frequency floor selects the right cards and you can afford the 2-source rule. In Vintage the two diverge: a Base Set Charizard sells a handful of times a month at five figures (enormous dollar volume, tiny count), while a $40 common churns daily. A frequency floor there would select the cheap commons and throw out the icons. PKMN-MDRN screens on frequency-plus-volume; PKMN-VNTG must screen on dollar volume alone. Same philosophy, opposite mechanics.

05Weighting & concentration control

Components are dollar-volume weighted: each name's weight is proportional to the value that actually traded through it over the trailing window. Dollar-volume weight (rather than pure population × price) is the honest choice for a hedge — it puts weight where you can actually put on and take off risk.

w_raw(i) = dollar_volume(i) / Σ dollar_volume // dollar_volume = trades × median price, 60d
w(i) = cap_waterfall( w_raw , 8% per card , 25% per franchise ) // iterative, renormalize to 1

Two caps, applied as an iterative waterfall (cap → redistribute → re-cap until stable):

  • 8% per card — no single slab can become the index.
  • 25% per franchise — every Charizard print, every Umbreon print, capped together. This is the cap that matters most: the chase market clusters hard around a few characters, and without it several Charizard prints would each sit near the single-card cap and collectively dominate.
The cap is the difference between a hedge and a leveraged Charizard bet. Uncapped, the top name would be ~21% of the modern level and the top five ~36%. Capped, the top name is 8% and the index carries an effective breadth of ~44 names — roughly double the uncapped figure.

06Settlement & the mark

The index settles on completed sales — never on asks, listings, or model "fair values." Each component's price is a winsorized, cluster-capped median of cross-venue completed sales, then 14-day smoothed before any return is taken. Smoothing-before-chaining is what stops a single high or low print from showing up as a fake index move.

// daily index level — geometric chained returns, weights fixed between rebalances
ret(t) = Σ w(i) · ln( mark(i,t) / mark(i,t-1) )
L(t) = L(t-1) · e^ret(t) // base level 1,000
  • Mark cadence: daily. Modern trades constantly, so a daily mark is real, not stale.
  • Rebalance: quarterly, with a divisor so adding/dropping a name never creates a phantom jump in the level.
  • New-set lag: 90 days. A new set's cards become eligible only three months after release — skipping the price-discovery chaos of launch week.

07Grade scope — why only PSA 9 and PSA 10

A grade earns a place only if it is (1) deep, (2) carried by many independent sources, and (3) a genuinely distinct risk factor rather than a condition-discounted copy of a card already in the index. For Modern, only PSA 9 and PSA 10 clear all three — they are the deepest, most source-independent (6–7 sources each), most standardized and most fungible grades in the market.

The source-count column is the lie detector: a grade carried by 6–7 venues is real signal; one carried by 1–2 is often a model-derived echo that would fake breadth.

08Risk profile & behaviour

The shape tells a story: modern front-loaded its gains 30–60 days ago and has since cooled to ~4% over the last month. Key statistical properties:

  • ~10–11% annualized volatility* — the lower-vol sleeve. Modern's enormous populations (Moonbreon's PSA-10 pop alone is ~21,000) and deep float diversify the index down.
  • Effective-N ≈ 44 — no single name drives it.
  • Correlation with PKMN-VNTG ≈ 0. Modern and Vintage are distinct risk factors, not two views of one market — which is exactly why they are two products. Use them as complementary exposures; a broad-hobby hedge is a blend of both, not either one alone.

09What's deliberately excluded

  • Sealed product own index — sealed is supply-driven (still being printed), trades on a different clock, and over recent windows has moved opposite to singles. It belongs in a separate PKMN-SEALED index, not bolted onto a singles benchmark.
  • Raw / NM own index — a different, retail-flavoured tier with condition ambiguity. The deepest modern tape by count, but kept separate so it can't inject grading noise.
  • CGC 10 / BGS / lower PSA redundant — correlated duplicates under different standards; breadth without a new risk factor.

10Data & integrity

Every component is an identity-gate-verified card with a crosswalk across venues, and every price is a completed sale drawn from the multi-source oracle (alt, TCGplayer, eBay, PokeTrace, Magic Eden, SNKRDUNK, Misprint and others). The ≥ 2 independent sources requirement on each modern component means no single feed — and no triangulated model price — can move the index by itself. Marks are winsorized and cluster-capped so a wash trade or fat-finger can't print into the level.
